# AI Risk Management

> "Risk should be managed before capability becomes consequence."

## Purpose

Defines the framework for identifying, assessing, mitigating, and monitoring risks introduced by AI systems.

## Philosophy

AI risk management should be continuous, proportional to impact, and integrated into architecture, development, deployment, and operations.

## Risk Lifecycle

1. Identify risk
2. Classify impact
3. Assess likelihood
4. Define controls
5. Assign ownership
6. Monitor
7. Review
8. Retire or revise controls

## Risk Categories

Assess:

- Safety
- Security
- Privacy
- Reliability
- Accuracy
- Compliance
- Operational
- Reputational

## Risk Classification

Consider:

- Impact severity
- Probability
- Reversibility
- Exposure
- User sensitivity
- Autonomy level

## Mitigation

Use:

- Preventive controls
- Detection controls
- Recovery controls
- Human escalation
- Reduced autonomy

## Risk Register

Maintain:

- Risk ID
- Description
- Owner
- Severity
- Likelihood
- Controls
- Residual risk
- Review date

## Monitoring

Track:

- New risks
- Control failures
- Incidents
- Residual risk
- Risk trends

## Governance

Require:

- Risk ownership
- Periodic assessments
- Approval thresholds
- Audit trails
- Incident linkage

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Treating risk assessment as a one-time activity
- Ignoring low-probability high-impact events
- Unowned risks
- Measuring risk without mitigation

## AI Context

AI coding agents should identify and document material risks before implementing autonomous or high-impact capabilities.
